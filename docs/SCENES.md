# Scenes

These are the signed-off layouts for every drill. The pitch is 105 m by 68 m. The learner's team attacks the top goal and defends the bottom goal. The build-out line is about 34.5 m from each goal line. Each scene uses only the players named. Good spots are fixed.

A move to a spot is a drag. A choice among marked targets is a pick.

Counter, react on turnover, and counter-press stay three drills.

## Starter drills

There are 20 starter drills.

### Open body

This is a drag. The learner is the receiver. The ball is with a teammate in the middle of the team's half. One defender stands on the learner's back.

The good spot is a step to the side of that defender, toward the open half of the pitch. From there the learner can face the ball and the top goal at once.

### Get open

This is a drag. The learner is a teammate off the ball. The ball is central. One opponent is touch-tight on the learner.

The good spot is the open pocket a few meters away from that opponent, where the passer can see the learner. Standing on the defender is the miss. Standing next to the ball is the miss.

### Stay wide

This is a drag. The learner is the wide player and is already near a touchline. The ball is in the middle. One teammate is already central.

The good spot stays on that touchline, level with the ball or slightly ahead of it. The learner does not run to the ball. Drifting into the crowd is the miss.

### Spread out

This is a drag. The learner is one of three teammates bunched on the ball in midfield. One opponent is near that bunch. The team uses height, width, and depth.

The good spot is the empty flank channel, well away from the other two. Staying in the bunch is the miss.

### Get wide

This is a drag. The learner starts in the central channel. The ball is stuck in that channel. One teammate is on the ball. One opponent blocks the middle.

The good spot is the open touchline on the weak side. The wide player stands in that outside channel. This is the move from the middle to the wing. Stay wide is the choice to remain there.

### Help the ball

This is a drag. The learner is the helper, not the ball carrier. A teammate has the ball. One opponent is pressuring that teammate.

The good spot is about 8 to 12 m to the side and slightly behind the ball, in view of the passer. A spot on top of the ball is the miss. A spot 30 m away is the miss.

### Pass and move

This is a drag of the run after the pass. The learner has just passed. The ball is now with a teammate. One opponent is near the spot the learner passed from.

The good spot is a new angle ahead of that pass. The spot the learner just left is the miss.

### Goal-side

This is a drag. The learner is a defender. The ball is at an opponent's feet, between the learner and the bottom goal.

The good spot is between that opponent and the bottom goal. A spot beside the opponent is the miss. A spot behind the ball is the miss.

### Ball-side

This is a drag. The learner is marking an opponent away from the ball. The ball is on the right.

The good spot is on the ball side of that opponent, and still goal-side, so the learner can see the ball and the player. The far shoulder, away from the ball, is the miss.

### Mark distance

This is a drag. The learner is marking one opponent who is not on the ball. The ball is about 15 m away.

The good spot is one to two meters off, goal-side and ball-side. Touch-tight is the miss. Ten meters off is the miss.

### Delay

This is a drag. The learner is the first defender. An opponent is dribbling at the bottom goal with the ball under control.

The good spot is a few meters goal-side, with the body sideways. A spot at the attacker's feet is the miss.

### Pressure and cover

This is a drag. The learner is the cover player, not the presser. A teammate is already on the ball. One opponent has the ball.

The good spot is a few meters behind that teammate and slightly inside, on the path to the bottom goal. A flat line next to the presser is the miss. That line leaves the middle open.

### Squeeze the middle

This is a drag. The learner is the weak-side defender. The ball is on the other wing. One teammate is pressing the ball.

The weak-side defender shifts toward the central channel. That shift is the good spot. Staying on the far touchline is the miss. Jumping all the way to the ball is the miss.

### Recovery run

This is a drag. The learner is caught upfield on the wing after a loss. The ball is with an opponent who is running at the bottom goal.

The good spot is a curved run back toward that goal. The run ends goal-side of the ball. A straight chase from behind is the miss.

### Goalkeeper step-out and line-up

This is one starter. It has three pictures. It is not three drills. The learner is the goalkeeper. The learner can switch angles before the drag. The angles are central, near post, and through-ball versus a set close shot. Near post is an angle in this drill. It is not a second drill. There is no outfield crowd. The central picture and the near-post picture each have one shooter. The through-ball picture has no shooter.

The central picture has the ball in front of the goal, outside the six-yard box. The good spot is one or two steps off the goal line, in the center of the goal, on the line from the ball to the middle of the goal. The penalty spot is the miss.

The near-post picture has the ball wide, near the corner of the penalty area. The good spot is toward the near post, a step or two off the goal line, about an arm's length from that post. The center of the goal is the miss. A spot outside the post is the miss.

The through-ball picture has one free ball only. The good spot is on the ball's path at the edge of the six-yard box.

A set close shot stays in the central and near-post pictures.

### Check away

This is a pick. The learner is the marked teammate. The ball is with a passer. One defender is tight.

The marks are a step toward the ball into the defender, a move away into the open space and then a show back, and standing still beside the defender. The correct mark is away into the open space, then showing back. A step into the defender leaves the learner marked. Standing still leaves the learner marked.

### Triangle

This is a pick. The learner chooses a spot around two teammates and the ball. One opponent is near the ball.

The marks are a straight line with the other two, the open corner that makes a triangle, and a spot next to the ball carrier. The correct mark is the open corner. The straight line gives one pass. A spot next to the ball gives no angle.

### Wall pass

This is a pick. The learner is the wide player and has just passed inside. The ball is with the inside teammate. One defender has stepped toward that teammate.

The marks are the space behind that defender on the safe side, the spot where the learner started, and a pass into the defender. The correct mark is the space behind the defender. The start spot is now marked. The defender's feet are not a pass.

### Switch the weak side

This is a pick. The learner chooses who receives. The ball is on the right. Two opponents are crowded on that side.

The marks are the marked player beside the ball, the open teammate on the left touchline, and a teammate in the middle of the crowd. The correct mark is the open teammate on the left touchline. The other two are where the opponents already are.

### Build-out

This is a pick. The learner is the goalkeeper. The ball is in the goalkeeper's hands. Opponents are behind the build-out line. Two teammates are free inside the team's half, one wide and one nearer the middle. A striker is beyond the build-out line.

The marks are the short pass to the open wide teammate, a long kick to the striker beyond the build-out line, and a pass into the space the opponents are about to enter. The correct mark is the short pass to the open wide teammate. The long kick gives the ball away.

## Next drills

There are 17 next drills. They stay locked until every starter is cleared.

### Diagonal run

This is a drag. The learner is the runner off the ball. A teammate has the ball wide. One defender is narrow.

The good spot is a diagonal run toward the corner, or into the gap behind that defender. A straight run at the ball is the miss.

### Numbers up

This is a drag. The learner is the free attacker. A teammate has the ball. One opponent is on that teammate.

The good spot is the open side of that defender, close enough for a 2v1. A spot next to the ball is the miss. A spot behind the defender is the miss.

### Track

This is a drag. The learner is a defender. The ball is at the side. An opponent runs from the box toward that ball.

The good spot is with that runner, goal-side, on the runner's path. Staying in the six-yard box and watching is the miss.

### Slide over

This is a drag. This is a step toward the middle. It is not a slide tackle. The learner is the far-side defender. The ball has moved from the left to the right. One teammate is now pressing the ball.

The good spot is a step toward the central channel on the ball side, still behind the presser. A dive at the ball carrier is the miss.

### Cover shadow

This is a drag. The learner is the presser. An opponent has the ball. A second opponent is in the middle, behind the learner.

The good spot is a curved step that puts the learner's body between the ball and the inside player. A straight run at the ball is the miss.

### Simple press triggers

This is a drag. It is one player and one spot. The learner is the nearest defender. An opponent has just taken a heavy touch and faces their own goal. The ball is with that opponent.

The good spot is a step to that ball. If the same opponent has the ball under control and faces forward, the spot stays the delay spot. That controlled ball is not this picture.

### Show outside

This is a drag. The learner is the first defender in the defending third. An opponent has the ball in the flank.

The good spot is on the inside shoulder, goal-side, so the easy path is the touchline. Standing square is the miss. That stance lets the opponent go either way. A spot that shows the opponent inside is the miss. That path opens the middle.

### Goalkeeper depth

This is a drag. The learner is the goalkeeper. The ball is in the opponent's half. It is not a shot.

The good spot is near the goal line, in the center of the goal. The penalty spot is the miss. A chip beats the goalkeeper there.

### Goalkeeper as plus-one

This is a drag. The learner is the goalkeeper. The team has the ball at the back. One defender is under pressure. Opponents are behind the build-out line.

The good spot is just outside the six-yard box, to the side of that defender, as a short passing option. A charge toward the halfway line is the miss.

### Counter

This is a drag. The learner is the free runner after the team wins the ball. One opponent is still upfield.

The good spot is toward the top goal, ahead of the ball. The picture stays one free runner. A square jog is the miss. A drop deeper is the miss.

### React on turnover

This is a drag. The learner is the nearest player after the team loses the ball. The new carrier is a few meters away.

The good spot is that nearest player's first step to the new carrier. A stop to watch is the miss. A sprint to the halfway line is the miss.

### Overlap

This is a pick. The learner is the supporting player. The learner starts narrower than a teammate who has the ball wide. One defender is on that teammate.

The marks are the outside path around the ball carrier, a run inside across the ball, and a spot behind the play. The correct mark is the outside path. The inside run repeats the ball carrier's lane. A spot behind the play does not make a 2v1.

### Diagonal ball

This is a pick. The learner chooses the pass. A teammate is making a straight run behind one defender.

The marks are the diagonal ball into that run, a square pass to a marked player, and a ball at the defender's feet. The correct mark is the diagonal ball into the run.

### Set and go

This is a pick. It has two pictures. One defender is tight.

In the setter picture, the learner has the ball. A teammate is running past. The setter's good spot is one touch into the runner's path.

In the runner picture, that ball is in the runner's path. The learner is the runner. The runner's good spot is arriving on that ball. Stopping where the runner started is the miss.

### Throw switch

This is a pick. It has two pictures. The throw is on the right touchline. The learner is the thrower.

In the first picture, a near player is open. The good mark is the short throw to that player. The long throw is the miss in this picture.

In the second picture, opponents mark the near side. One teammate is open on the far side. The good mark is the long throw to that open far player. The short throw to the marked near side is the miss in this picture.

### Corner near and far

This is a pick. It has one picture. The corner is from the right. The learner is the runner. The ball is played past the near-post defender.

The one correct spot is the far-post space inside the six-yard box. The near post, the penalty spot, and the edge of the box are misses. The near post is already behind the ball. The penalty spot is too deep for this first ball. The edge of the box is a second ball, not the run.

### Short corner

This is a pick. It has one picture. The corner is from the right. The learner takes the corner. The box is crowded. One teammate is free just outside the corner arc.

The correct mark is that free teammate. A kick into the near-post crowd is the miss.

## Advanced drills

There are 8 advanced drills. They stay locked until every next drill is cleared.

### Offside line step or drop

This is a drag. The pitch is 7v7. It has two pictures. The learner is a defender on the build-out line. One teammate is on that line with the learner. The line is the build-out line. It is not the halfway line. This is not a trap.

When the ball is safe, the good spot is a step up onto the build-out line with that teammate. In that picture the ball is at an opponent's feet, and that opponent faces their own goal.

When the ball is played in behind, the good spot is a drop goal-side of the runner, toward the team's penalty area.

Holding the line to catch the runner is the trap. That picture is out of scope.

### Zones

This is a drag. It has two pictures. The learner is a defender for the central channel in front of the bottom goal. A wide runner goes toward the wing.

If another defender can take that runner, the learner stays central and goal-side. Chasing the runner to the touchline is the miss. That chase leaves the middle empty.

If the learner is closest and the other defenders can slide into the middle, the learner goes with the runner. Staying central in this picture is the miss.

### Rest defense

This is a drag. One player stays while two teammates attack the top goal with the ball. The learner is the player who stays.

The good spot is goal-side of the ball, central, near the halfway line. Joining the attack is the miss. Dropping to the goal line is the miss.

### Counter-press

This is a drag. It is one player and one spot. The team has just lost the ball in the opponent's half. The learner is the nearest player there. One opponent has just received the ball and faces their own goal. One teammate is the cover player.

The good spot cuts the forward pass. Dropping to the build-out line is the miss.

### Between the lines

This is a drag. The learner is an attacker. Two opponents form a line in midfield. Two more opponents form a line nearer their goal. The ball is with a teammate behind the first line.

The good spot is the pocket between those two lines, where the passer can see the learner. A spot beyond the last line is the miss.

### Third man

This is a pick. The learner chooses who receives the third pass. One player has the ball. A second player can set it. One defender steps to the setter.

The marks are the third player arriving in the space that step opened, the original passer, and a player standing in the defender's shadow. The original passer is now marked. The correct mark is the third player in the opened space.

### Far-post run

This is a pick. A teammate is about to cross from the right. One teammate is already at the near post. The learner chooses the run.

The marks are the far post, arriving as the cross comes, the near post, and a spot next to the crosser. The near post is already taken. The correct mark is the far post.

### Swap places

This is a pick. The picture is attacking. A teammate overlaps outside with the ball. The learner is the wide player in that path.

The wide player pinches into the inside spot that teammate left. That inside spot is the correct mark. Staying in the same lane is the miss. Stopping in front of the overlap is the miss.
